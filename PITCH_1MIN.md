# Gemba, in one minute

*Spoken at a normal pace this is about 60 seconds. Console actions in brackets.*

Every warehouse change gets planned on a spreadsheet and then survives contact with reality in a daily standup. Simulators model the floor. Nobody models the meeting. [floor view]

Gemba does both. A deterministic twin of the warehouse, and an org of AI agents that run it: a program manager, ops, safety with a veto, quality with a lock, a shift lead who owns the labor truth, a director for escalations. Every morning they hold a standup, argue with simulator evidence, and act through typed levers. Then the floor answers. [meeting room, a decision landing]

That is the difference. In a normal simulation a decision is an input. Here a decision has consequences, and consequences trigger the next decision. An aggressive night of moves causes a picker learning dip, mis-picks, a key-customer escalation, Quality in the room, SKUs locked, the reslot slowed. You can see the whole chain, and click back to the decision that started it. [cascade trace]

So engineers rehearse instead of hoping. Run the month before you live it. Fork any day with a different call, "protect the Pick Module all week", and watch the other future next to this one. Same month, customer-first versus efficiency-first: you see what each priority costs. Type a disruption in plain words and the org re-plans. [fork, compare, inbox]

And nothing is lost when people leave. Every option, every objection, every number is in the decision record. The org even reviews its own worst days, proves a better move by forking, and keeps the lesson only if it wins on scenarios it has never seen. [playbook rule and its fork]

Today the scenario is a warehouse reslot. The pattern is any operational change: a peak season, a WMS cutover, a new customer. Swap the twin, keep the room. Gemba, on Nemotron.
